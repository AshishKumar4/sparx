## The task

A car carries an event camera that looks at the road ahead. The road is dark, its verges are light, and a dashed line runs down its middle. The camera sends nothing about the road itself, only where its brightness changes as the car moves, and a car that stands still sees nothing at all. From those events and its own speed, a network of spiking neurons must steer and set the speed, around a track it has never seen.

Nobody shows it how. Event-camera steering has been learned by imitation: Maqueda et al. trained a conventional network to predict a human driver's steering from a large recorded dataset. The racer has no driver to imitate, and learns from what happens when it drives.

## The car and its camera

The car is a kinematic bicycle: a wheelbase $L$ of 0.4 m, steering $\delta$ of at most 0.6 rad, a top speed of 6 m/s. Every 20 ms the network's two readout membranes $u_1$ and $u_2$ set the steering and a target speed, and the car moves:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\delta = 0.6 \tanh u_1, \qquad v^\ast = 6\,\sigma(u_2) \\
v \leftarrow v + \frac{\Delta t}{0.5}\,(v^\ast - v), \qquad \psi \leftarrow \psi + \Delta t\, \frac{v \tan\delta}{L} \\
(x, y) \leftarrow (x, y) + \Delta t\, v\, (\cos\psi, \sin\psi)
\end{gathered}
$$

The camera has 24 × 12 pixels, each looking at one point of the ground: rows from 0.6 m to 7 m ahead, spaced as a real camera's rows fall on a flat road, each spanning 0.9 times its distance to either side. Each pixel is chapter 12's: it sends an ON or OFF event when the logarithm of the brightness it sees has moved 0.15 from the level of its last event. The network reads 577 numbers each step, the 288 pixels' ON events, their 288 OFF events and the car's speed, through two layers of LIF neurons, 96 and 64, into the two readout integrators.

## Gradients through a camera

The car, the camera and the network are written in JAX, so a drive is one differentiable function of the network's weights. Most of it is smooth: the car's motion, the brightness of the ground, its logarithm. Two kinds of step are not, the neurons' spikes and the pixels' events, and both are `sparx.spike`, with chapter 5's surrogate for a derivative. The pixels use a narrower one than the neurons, since their threshold is 0.15 rather than 1.

So the gradient of the loss reaches the weights by every path there is: through the readouts and the spikes, into the events, through each pixel's threshold into the brightness it saw, through the ground into the car's position and heading, and back through every earlier step of the drive. Here is one pixel row's piece of that chain:

```
move 0.05 m: 1 ON events, gradient 29.3 per m
move 0.10 m: 3 ON events, gradient 15.2 per m
move 0.20 m: 3 ON events, gradient 8.0 per m
move 0.40 m: 5 ON events, gradient 5.6 per m
```

The count of events is a staircase in the car's position, flat between its steps, so its true derivative is zero almost everywhere. The surrogate's is never zero: between 0.1 and 0.2 m the count stays at 3, and the gradient still says that moving further brings more events.

## The loss

Each training step draws 32 tracks like those you see, puts a car on each at a random point, off the middle, askew and moving, and drives them all for 300 steps, 6 s. With $d$ the distance from the middle of the road, $w = 0.8$ m its half width, $\dot s$ the speed along the road and $r_i$ the share of steps on which neuron $i$ fired:

$$
L = \Big\langle \frac{d^2}{w^2} \Big\rangle + 4\, \big\langle (d - w)_+^2 \big\rangle - 0.5\, \Big\langle \frac{\dot s}{6} \Big\rangle + 2\, \Big\langle \frac{(-\dot s)_+}{6} \Big\rangle + 10\, \big\langle (r_i - 0.3)_+^2 + (0.02 - r_i)_+^2 \big\rangle
$$

The first two terms keep the car on the road, the second hard once it is off. The third rewards progress, the fourth punishes driving the wrong way round, and the last keeps every neuron in use and none saturated, as in the drone. Adam follows the gradient, clipped to a norm of 1, with a learning rate that warms up and then decays.

Three more choices, with the longer drive and the fourth term, made this training reliable; the next section shows what they did together. The gradient reaches back at most 50 steps, 1 s: every 50 steps the drive's state is cut from its past, as chapter 6's streaming layers cut it between chunks. The tracks start gentle, at a bend strength of 0.3, and bend harder over the first half of training, to 1.3, sharper than the 0.9 of the tracks it is tested on first. And every 250 steps the network drives 32 tracks of its own for 20 s, never the tracks it is tested on, and the run keeps the weights that drove farthest.

## How often it failed, and what fixed it

The first version of this training drove 150 steps, 3 s, with the gradient carried back through all of them, on tracks of one difficulty, and kept its last weights. Gradients carried back through 150 steps of a car, a camera and a network explode, as chapter 6 warned, and it did not survive them often. Each row is 3,000 training steps at a learning rate of 0.001 and a batch of 32, five seeds of each recipe, scored on 200 tracks it never trained on:

| Recipe | Seeds that finished every track | Unseen tracks finished, seeds 1 to 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The first: 150 steps, no cut, last weights | 1 of 5 | 200, 2, 3, 94 and 25 of 200 |
| 300 steps cut every 50, bends grown, wrong way punished | 3 of 5 | 200, 200, 134, 163 and 200 of 200 |
| The same, keeping the weights that drove farthest on held-out tracks | 5 of 5 | 200 of 200 each |

Under the first recipe the median gradient norm before clipping was 97 for seed 1 and between 1,600 and 6.8 million for the other four, and the largest reached $2.6 \times 10^{15}$. Clipping holds the step's size, but its direction comes from whichever path exploded: seed 2 learned to drive backwards. Under the second recipe, which cuts the gradient every 50 steps, the median norm was between 0.1 and 38. A cut bounds the chain of factors a gradient is multiplied through, but the second recipe changed four things at once, and these runs do not say how much each did. Two of those five seeds then drove well halfway through training and worse by its end, once the tracks reached their hardest bends; choosing the weights on tracks of their own recovered both. Keeping the weights that score best on held-out data is ordinary practice, and here the held-out tracks are never the ones the network is tested on. Of the five networks the last recipe trained, the one that drove farthest on its held-out tracks is the car on this page and the front page.

## How well it drives

Each of 200 tracks it never trained on, drawn by the same rule with another seed, starts the car at rest in the middle of the road, pointing along it, and gives it 30 s. A lap counts when the car has gone the track's length without straying more than a metre beyond the road's edge.

## Try this

1. Every drive starts with the car at rest. What does the network see at the first step, and how does the car get moving?
2. Draw a track with a hairpin much tighter than any in training. Why might the car fail there, when it drives the 200 unseen tracks without a fault?
3. Why does the loss reward speed along the road, rather than speed itself?
4. The last recipe keeps the weights that drove farthest on 32 tracks of their own. Why not choose them on the 200 tracks it is scored on?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Nothing: a still camera sends no events, and the speed it reads is 0. But the target speed is $6\,\sigma(u_2)$, which is never zero, so the car starts to roll, and as it moves the dashes and the road's edges start to send events.
2. The 200 unseen tracks were drawn by the same rule as the training tracks, so their bends are no sharper than those it learned on. A hairpin asks for slowing and steering it never needed in training, and nothing in the loss taught it what to do there.
3. Speed itself can be had by driving in circles, or straight off the track. Speed along the road counts only progress round the track.
4. Weights chosen on the 200 tracks would be those that happened to suit those tracks, so their score would overstate how the network drives tracks it has never seen. Tracks of their own give the choice its own data, and the 200 stay unseen until the end, as a test set must.

</details>

## Summary

A spiking network learned to drive from an event camera's output and its own speed, by gradients through its spikes, the car's motion and the pixels' own thresholds, and finished every one of 200 tracks it had never seen. Its first recipe worked for one seed in five. Cutting the gradient every 50 steps, growing the bends over training and keeping the weights that drove farthest on held-out tracks made it work for all five.
